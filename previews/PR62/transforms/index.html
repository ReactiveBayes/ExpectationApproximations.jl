Constrained variables

A Gaussian has the whole real line as its support, but many variables are constrained: a precision is positive, a probability lies between zero and one, and mixture weights sum to one. A Gaussian placed directly on such a variable puts mass on values the variable cannot take. Its Laplace approximation can even be centred at a mode on the boundary, as for a Beta density that is infinite at zero.

The usual remedy is to approximate in unconstrained coordinates. Write $x = g(u)$ for a smooth bijection $g$ from the real line onto the domain, approximate the density of $u$ by a Gaussian, and push it forward through $g$. The density of $u$ gains the log-Jacobian of $g$:

\[\log p_u(u) = \log p_x(g(u)) + \log \left|\det \frac{\partial g}{\partial u}\right|.\]

using ExpectationApproximations, Distributions
import BayesBase
const EA = ExpectationApproximations

The domain of a distribution

domain reads the domain from the support of a distribution, usually the prior of the variable. A model can then keep priors such as a Gamma, a Beta or a Dirichlet, and the transform is used only in the approximation:

EA.domain(Gamma(2.0, 1.0)), EA.domain(Beta(1.0, 1.0)), EA.domain(Uniform(-1.0, 3.0)), EA.domain(Dirichlet(3, 1.0))
(ExpectationApproximations.Positive(), ExpectationApproximations.UnitInterval(), ExpectationApproximations.Bounded{Float64}(-1.0, 3.0), ExpectationApproximations.Simplex(3))

Approximations in a domain

laplace, moment_matching and gaussian_variational take a domain as their third argument. Take a Gamma posterior for a rate, $\mathrm{Gamma}(23, 1/21)$:

a, b = 23.0, 21.0
result = EA.laplace(x -> logpdf(Gamma(a, 1 / b), x), 1.0, EA.Positive())
result.distribution
TransformedGaussian distribution
Parameters:
  gaussian = Normal(0.09097177824702057, 0.20851441405276958)
  domain   = ExpectationApproximations.Positive()

The result is a TransformedGaussian: a Gaussian in $u = \log x$, here $\mathcal{N}(\log(a/b), 1/a)$, pushed forward onto the positive half-line. It has a logpdf and can be sampled. Its mean, variance and entropy are computed by quadrature over its Gaussian:

mean(result.distribution), mean(Gamma(a, 1 / b))
(1.119308303857624, 1.0952380952380951)

GaussHermite, and so EADefault, computes expectations under it through its Gaussian:

BayesBase.expectation(EA.GaussHermite(16), log, result.distribution)
0.09097177824702057

In a domain, logZ approximates the log-normaliser in the original coordinates. The log-Jacobian accounts for the change of variables, so for a normalised density logZ is close to zero. The mode field of laplace and the mean field of the other two are the images $g(\hat u)$ of the unconstrained mode or mean. They are not the mode or mean of the returned distribution, because a nonlinear transform moves both:

result.logZ, result.mode, a / b
(-0.0036229602453158316, 1.0952380952833218, 1.0952380952380953)

A density that is infinite at the boundary, such as $\mathrm{Beta}(0.8, 3)$ at zero, has no mode inside the unit interval, but in logit coordinates it has one:

beta = EA.laplace(x -> logpdf(Beta(0.8, 3.0), x), 0.5, EA.UnitInterval())
beta.converged, mean(beta.distribution)
(true, 0.2649948558852191)

A block of parameters in different domains, such as a probability and a rate that are correlated in the posterior, has a Componentwise domain. It is approximated jointly by one Gaussian in two unconstrained coordinates.

ExpectationApproximations.Domain — Type
Domain

The set of values of a random variable, with a smooth bijection forward from unconstrained coordinates onto it, its inverse, and the log-Jacobian logjacobian of forward. A Gaussian in the unconstrained coordinates, pushed forward through forward, approximates a density on the domain.

source
ExpectationApproximations.RealLine — Type
RealLine()

The whole real line, or ℝᵈ for a vector: the domain of a Gaussian. The transform is the identity, so an approximation in this domain is the same as one without a domain.

source
ExpectationApproximations.Positive — Type
Positive()

The positive half-line, x > 0, through x = exp(u): the domain of a Gamma, an inverse Gamma or a log-normal variable, such as a rate, a precision or a scale. A Gaussian in u is a log-normal distribution in x.

source
ExpectationApproximations.UnitInterval — Type
UnitInterval()

The open unit interval, 0 < x < 1, through x = logistic(u): the domain of a Beta variable, such as a probability. A Gaussian in u is a logit-normal distribution in x.

source
ExpectationApproximations.LowerBounded — Type
LowerBounded(lower)

The half-line x > lower, through x = lower + exp(u).

source
ExpectationApproximations.UpperBounded — Type
UpperBounded(upper)

The half-line x < upper, through x = upper - exp(u).

source
ExpectationApproximations.Bounded — Type
Bounded(lower, upper)

The interval lower < x < upper, through x = lower + (upper - lower) logistic(u): a UnitInterval scaled and shifted.

source
ExpectationApproximations.Simplex — Type
Simplex(K)

The probability vectors of length K, through the additive log-ratio transform from K - 1 unconstrained coordinates: xₖ = exp(uₖ) / (1 + Σⱼ exp(uⱼ)) for k < K.

source
ExpectationApproximations.Componentwise — Type
Componentwise(domains...)

A vector whose components lie in different domains, each transformed separately, such as a block of correlated parameters made of a probability and a rate.

source
ExpectationApproximations.domain — Function
domain(q)

The Domain of the distribution q, read from its support, or nothing when no domain of this package matches the support, as for a discrete distribution. For the BayesBase log-densities ContinuousUnivariateLogPdf and ContinuousMultivariateLogPdf, the domain is read from their own domain, and is nothing when that is unspecified. For a product of densities, such as the product of a prior and messages, it is the narrowest of its factors' domains: the intersection of their intervals, where the real line of a Gaussian factor, such as a message moment-matched through a function, does not hide the support of another factor.

source
ExpectationApproximations.forward — Function
forward(domain, u)

The value in domain of the unconstrained coordinates u: exp(u) for Positive, logistic(u) for UnitInterval, and so on. It is a smooth bijection from the real line (or ℝᵈ) onto the domain, so forward(domain, u) is in the domain for every finite u.

julia> EA.forward(EA.Positive(), 0.0)
1.0

julia> EA.forward(EA.Simplex(3), [0.0, 0.0]) ≈ [1, 1, 1] / 3
true
source
ExpectationApproximations.inverse — Function
inverse(domain, x)

The unconstrained coordinates of the value x in domain, so that forward(domain, inverse(domain, x)) ≈ x: log(x) for Positive, logit(x) for UnitInterval, and so on. For a Simplex of K components, a vector of K - 1.

julia> EA.inverse(EA.UnitInterval(), 0.5)
0.0
source
ExpectationApproximations.logjacobian — Function
logjacobian(domain, u)

log |det ∂forward(domain, u) / ∂u|, the term that a log-density gains in unconstrained coordinates: a density p(x) on the domain is the density p(forward(domain, u)) |det ∂forward/∂u| of u. For Positive, logjacobian(Positive(), u) = u, since d exp(u) / du = exp(u). For a Simplex, it is the log-Jacobian of the first K - 1 components, which are free.

julia> EA.logjacobian(EA.Positive(), 0.7)
0.7
source
ExpectationApproximations.pushforward — Function
pushforward(domain, gaussian)

The distribution of forward(domain, u) for u ~ gaussian. On the real line this is gaussian itself. On any other domain it is a TransformedGaussian, including the log-normal distribution on the positive half-line and the logit-normal distribution on the unit interval. Distributions' LogNormal and LogitNormal are not used, because they have no mean(log, q) in the ecosystem, which conjugate rules use, and LogitNormal also has no mean, variance or entropy. A TransformedGaussian has all of them.

source
ExpectationApproximations.TransformedGaussian — Type
TransformedGaussian(gaussian, domain)

The distribution of forward(domain, u) for u ~ gaussian: a Gaussian in unconstrained coordinates, pushed forward onto a Domain such as the positive half-line, an interval, a simplex or a block of components in different domains. It has a logpdf and can be sampled. GaussHermite computes its expectations through its Gaussian.
