ExpectationApproximations

Approximate inference spends most of its time on two questions:

  • What is the expectation of a function? For example $\mathbb{E}_q[f(x)]$ under a distribution $q$ that has no closed form for it: $\mathbb{E}[\log x]$ under a Gamma, $\mathbb{E}[\sigma(a + b x)]$ under a Gaussian, the expected log-likelihood of a model's factor.
  • Which Gaussian best represents a density? Approximate inference often has to replace an intractable density by a Gaussian: at its mode (Laplace), with its moments (expectation propagation) or as the Gaussian closest to it (variational inference).

ExpectationApproximations answers both, numerically. It provides:

  • backends that compute expectations by quadrature, cubature, sigma points, exact sums or sampling, through the expectation protocol of BayesBase;
  • Gaussian approximations of a density given as a Julia function: laplace, moment_matching, gaussian_variational, and the moments of functions of random variables;
  • domains and transforms, so a variable that must be positive, a probability or a probability vector is approximated by a Gaussian in unconstrained coordinates.

A first example

The expected logarithm of a Gamma variable has no elementary closed form in most libraries, but Gauss–Laguerre quadrature computes it accurately with a handful of points:

using ExpectationApproximations, Distributions
import BayesBase
const EA = ExpectationApproximations

q = Gamma(3.0, 2.0)
BayesBase.expectation(EA.GaussLaguerre(32), log, q)
1.615962674331821

The exact value is $\psi(3) + \log 2$:

using SpecialFunctions: digamma
digamma(3.0) + log(2.0)
1.6159315156584126

Most of the time one does not pick the rule by hand. EADefault sends each distribution to the rule made for its family, and a backend can be prepared once for a distribution and then evaluated for many functions, without allocating:

prepared = BayesBase.prepare_expectation(EA.EADefault(), q)
BayesBase.expectation(prepared, x -> x^2), BayesBase.expectation(prepared, sqrt)
(48.00000000000001, 2.3499906129124475)

And a Gaussian approximation of a density is one call. Here, the Laplace approximation of a skewed density on the real line:

result = EA.laplace(x -> x - exp(x) / 2, 0.0)
result.distribution, result.converged
(Normal(0.6931471805600254, 0.9999999999999599), true)

Where it fits

ExpectationApproximations is one layer of the ReactiveBayes ecosystem:

PackageRole
BayesBasedefines the expectation protocol: expectation, prepare_expectation, and how a backend says what it supports
ExpectationApproximationsimplements backends for the protocol, and the Gaussian approximations built on them
DeltaMessagePassingRulesturns these approximations into message passing rules for nodes that have no hand-written ones
RxInferruns inference on models without hand-written rules with infer(...; fallback = LocalLaplace()), CVI(), LaplacePropagation() or ExpectationPropagation()

Nothing in this package depends on message passing: the backends and the approximations work on any distribution and any Julia function.

Installation

import Pkg
Pkg.add("ExpectationApproximations")

Nothing is exported: every name is public and used qualified, as in the examples above, usually with const EA = ExpectationApproximations. The functions of the expectation protocol, expectation and prepare_expectation, are BayesBase's.

ExpectationApproximations.ExpectationApproximations — Module
ExpectationApproximations

Numerical backends for the expectation protocol of BayesBase: quadrature, cubature, sigma points and sampling compute E_q[f] through BayesBase.expectation and BayesBase.prepare_expectation. On top of them, the Gaussian approximations of a density that approximate inference builds on (Laplace, moment matching, the variational Gaussian, linearisation), and transforms to unconstrained coordinates for variables with a constrained domain.

Nothing is exported: the names are public and used qualified, such as ExpectationApproximations.GaussHermite.
