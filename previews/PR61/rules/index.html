Quadrature rules

The quadrature backends are built on four classical Gaussian rules. Each approximates an integral against a weight function by a weighted sum over $n$ nodes, exactly for polynomials up to degree $2n - 1$:

RuleIntegralUsed by
gausshermite$\int f(x)\, e^{-x^2} dx$GaussHermite, for Gaussians
gausslaguerre$\int_0^\infty f(x)\, x^{\alpha} e^{-x} dx$GaussLaguerre, for Gammas of any shape
gaussjacobi$\int_{-1}^1 f(x)\, (1 - x)^\alpha (1 + x)^\beta dx$GaussJacobi, for Betas
gausslegendre$\int_{-1}^1 f(x)\, dx$QuantileLegendre, for any continuous distribution
using ExpectationApproximations
const EA = ExpectationApproximations
nodes, weights = EA.gausshermite(5)
sum(weights .* nodes .^ 4), 3 * sqrt(π) / 4   # ∫ x⁴ e^{-x²} dx
(1.329340388179137, 1.329340388179137)

How they are computed

The rules are computed with the Golub–Welsch algorithm. The nodes are the eigenvalues of the Jacobi matrix, the symmetric tridiagonal matrix of the orthogonal polynomials' three-term recurrence, polished by Newton's method on that recurrence. The weights are the Christoffel numbers $1 / \sum_k p_k(x_i)^2$ of the orthonormal polynomials.

Computing the weights this way, rather than from the first components of the eigenvectors, keeps the smallest weights accurate to about $10^{-13}$ of their size against 256-bit arithmetic, out in the tails where the eigenvectors lose relative precision. The rules are computed in Float64 when a backend is constructed or, for Gauss–Laguerre and Gauss–Jacobi, whose weight depends on the shape, when an expectation is prepared.
